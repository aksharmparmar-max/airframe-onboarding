01

Start here

Welcome to Airframe. We design a VTOL aircraft — it takes off and lands straight up and down like a helicopter, then flies efficiently like a plane in between. The airframe is the structural skeleton: the parts that hold the motors, electronics, and payload together and keep the whole thing rigid in flight.

The part you're building is the Motor Mount — the bracket that attaches a brushless motor to that skeleton. It's small, but it carries real flight loads, so the fit has to be right. That makes it a good first project: you'll learn the tools and the way we actually design hardware here.

Read this before you start

This is a learning project, not a test. Your first CAD model almost certainly won't match the production part — that's expected. What matters is the process: taking measurements, thinking through a design, getting comfortable with the tools.

Ask questions early and often. Five minutes of help beats an hour stuck alone.

What you'll walk away with

  • Fusion 360 fluencyComfort navigating the interface and the core modeling tools.
  • Reading a real assemblyThe ability to open a CAD assembly and understand how the parts fit together.
  • Designing to an interfaceExperience making a part that has to mate precisely with another part — a core airframe skill.
  • A real componentA model of a part that's genuinely on our aircraft.
  • A way inA clear sense of who to ask for help and how the subteam's CAD workflow runs.

File formats

The files in the shared folder are Fusion 360 native (.f3d parts and an .f3z assembly), which open directly in Fusion — upload them to your project through the Data Panel.

If you're on SolidWorks, Onshape, or anything else, ask Akshar for .step exports instead. STEP is the universal format any CAD program can read, and it's what we use to share parts between software and with manufacturers.

09

Stuck?

Things every first-time Fusion 360 user runs into. Try these first if you want — but posting in the Airframe Slack channel is always a fine move too.

My sketch won't fully constrain, and I don't know why.

Check the status bar at the bottom of the screen — Fusion tells you how many degrees of freedom remain. Under-constrained lines usually show in a different color. Add dimensions or geometric constraints (parallel, coincident, and so on) one at a time until it goes fully constrained.

Fusion says my sketch is over-constrained or conflicting.

You've added a redundant or contradictory dimension. Undo your most recent constraint and try a different one — you often only need one more constraint than you think.

I can't find the right plane to sketch on.

Use the assembly's existing faces as sketch planes where you can — click a flat face, then start a new sketch — rather than creating new offset planes. It keeps things simpler while you're learning.

My interference check shows a collision I don't understand.

Zoom into the flagged area and toggle component visibility on and off to see exactly which two faces overlap. It's usually a joint positioned slightly wrong.

I'm stuck and none of this helped.

Post a screenshot in the Airframe Slack channel and tag Akshar or a mentor. Getting unstuck in five minutes with help beats an hour stuck alone — asking questions is expected, not a last resort.

Glossary

Come back here any time something trips you up. Nothing to memorize.

CAD
Computer-Aided Design — software used to design 3D parts before they're built.
Fusion 360
The CAD software our team uses to design parts and assemblies.
STEP file (.step)
A universal 3D format any CAD program can open — how we share parts between software and with manufacturers.
Sketch
A 2D drawing (lines, circles) that you turn into a 3D shape, usually by extruding or revolving it.
Constraint / fully constrained
Rules that lock down a sketch's size and position so it can't move unexpectedly. Fully constrained means every line and dimension is locked — nothing left loose or ambiguous.
Extrude
Pushing a flat 2D sketch outward to create a 3D solid — like stamping a cookie-cutter shape and giving it thickness.
Revolve
Spinning a 2D sketch around an axis to create a round 3D shape, like a lathe.
Fillet
A rounded edge added to a sharp corner — for looks, strength, or to avoid sharp edges.
Feature tree / timeline
The list at the bottom of the Fusion window showing every step you took to build the part, in order.
Assembly
Multiple parts (components) combined and positioned relative to each other.
Joint
How two components are connected, and whether they're allowed to move relative to each other.
Interference check
A tool that checks whether two parts in an assembly overlap or collide where they shouldn't.
Bolt pattern / bolt circle
The arrangement — usually a ring — of holes used to bolt two parts together.
VTOL
Vertical Take-Off and Landing. Our aircraft lifts off straight up like a helicopter, then flies like a plane.
